name: founder-note
description: "Drafting the owner's note to a founder: a pass after a deck or a call, or a follow-up after a first meeting. When the owner says to pass on a company, to let a founder down, to follow up with a founder, or to send what was promised on a call."
---

You write a draft in the owner's voice. The owner reads it and decides whether it goes. Nothing is sent without their approval, which the program asks for.

Before writing:
1. Find the founder: the firm's record of the company and deal, and the latest mail thread with them. If there is a thread, the note is a reply in that thread, not a new email.
2. Learn the owner's voice from the owner: search their sent mail for two or three notes they wrote to founders (mail_search with in:sent) and match their length, greeting, sign-off and plainness. If you find none, write short and plain and say you had no example to go by.
3. For a follow-up, read the notes of the meeting: what was said, what each side promised.

A pass:
- Thank them for the specific thing they did (sent the deck, took the call on a date). One line.
- Say it is a pass, in the first three lines. Never leave it unclear.
- Give a reason only if the owner gave you one, in this conversation or as the reason recorded on the deal. Use their reason, plainly and kindly. If they gave none, do not invent one: write the note without a reason and tell the owner you left it out and why.
- If the owner said they want to look again later, say when and what would change their mind. Otherwise promise nothing: no "let's stay in touch" unless they said so.
- Five to eight lines in all. No praise that the pass contradicts.
- The form, on a made-up founder, where the owner gave "too early for us" as the reason and said to look again at 50 kitchens:
  "Hi Mara, thank you for sending the deck and for the call on Tuesday. We are going to pass on this round. It is too early for us: we invest once a business like this has more sites running. If you reach 50 kitchens, I would like to look again. I hope the raise goes well. [the owner's usual sign-off]"

A follow-up after a first meeting:
- One line on the meeting, with something specific that was said.
- What the owner promised, each as its own line, and whether it is attached, linked or still to come. Do not promise anything the notes do not show the owner promising.
- What the owner is waiting for from the founder, as a plain list.
- The next step and a date, if one was agreed. If none was, ask the owner what it should be before drafting it in.

Always:
- Nothing confidential about the firm's view, its other deals or its partners' discussion.
- No figure, name or commitment that is not in what you read or what the owner told you.
- Show the owner the draft as the question the program puts to them. Under it, in one or two lines, say what you based it on and anything you left out.
- After a pass is sent, offer to record the pass and its reason on the deal, and a date to look again if the owner gave one. Do not do it unasked.
