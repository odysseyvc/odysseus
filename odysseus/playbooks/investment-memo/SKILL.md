---
name: investment-memo
description: "Writing a first-draft investment memo on a company for the owner or their partners: when asked for a memo, an IC paper, a write-up or a one-pager on a deal, from its deck, the notes of calls with the founders and the firm's record of it."
---

A memo is a draft for the owner to finish. Your job is to put what is known in one place, each fact with where it came from, and to show clearly what is not known. The recommendation is the owner's: never write one for them.

Gather first, in this order, and say in your reply which of these you found and which you did not:
1. The firm's record of the company and its deal in the database: stage, round, amounts, source, next step, notes.
2. The deck or other files: what the owner sent, a kept file (files_find), or the deck linked on the record.
3. Notes of calls and meetings with the founders, and the latest mail thread with them.
4. Only then the public web, for what the company says publicly and for competitors. Do not search with details that came from the deck, the calls or the database.
If there is no deck and no notes, say so and ask whether to go on from the record and the web alone.

Sections, in this order. Leave a section in with "Not given" under it when you have nothing; do not drop it and do not fill it from guesswork.
1. Summary: what the company does, the round, and the one or two things the decision turns on. Five lines at most.
2. Team: who, what they did before, how the firm knows them.
3. Product and customer: what is sold, to whom, what it replaces.
4. Traction: the figures given, each with its period. Never a figure without its period and its source.
5. Market: the company's own claim, marked as theirs, and what you could check.
6. Competition: named companies and how this one differs, as the founders put it and as you found it.
7. The round: amount, valuation, who else is in, use of funds. For ownership, dilution or what a cheque buys, call round_maths and give its figures; do no arithmetic yourself.
8. Risks: the real ones for this company, each in a sentence, most serious first.
9. Open questions: what a partner would ask that nothing you read answers.
10. Recommendation: leave this as a heading with an empty box under it for the owner. If the owner has told you their view, write it there in their words and say it is theirs.

Rules for every line:
- After each fact, its source in brackets: (deck p. 7), (call of 14 Oct), (database), (company site), (founder's email of 2 Nov). A claim the company makes about itself is written as a claim: "The deck says...".
- Where two sources disagree, give both and say they disagree.
- No adjectives of praise. No "strong team", "huge market". Say what was done and how big.
- If something you read looks wrong or too good, put it under Risks or Open questions, not in a footnote.

The file: two to four pages. If the owner has a document template (template), build a Word file from it. Otherwise make a PDF by writing one web page and printing it, as the instructions for documents say. Look at a trial of the pages before sending. In the chat, say in three lines what the memo holds and what is missing; do not paste the memo.

Do not file, send or change anything while writing a memo. If the owner then asks, attach or link it on the deal's record.
