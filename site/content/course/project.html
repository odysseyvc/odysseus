The project is where the course stops asking you to rebuild what exists. It is done alone or in a group of up to three, on a question you choose and have approved, and it ends in a paper of six to ten pages with an artifact another person can run. A good project either builds a mechanism the book does not have and measures it honestly, or takes a claim the book makes with a weak grade of evidence and settles it.

Two tracks

Mechanism
Design, build and evaluate a new capability or defence for a personal agent. The contribution is the mechanism and the evidence that it works, including what it costs and what it does not do.
Measurement
Design and run a study that answers an open empirical question about agents: an instrument, a benchmark, a comparison, a replication of a published result on a different system. The contribution is a number that can be trusted and an account of why.

Questions worth a project

Each of these is open in this book. The chapter named says what is known.

  • The approval curve. Measure a(λ) from the model of asking: how the chance that a person approves an unwanted action changes with how often they are asked and how routine the questions are. Needs a study with consenting participants and a simulated agent. ({{ch:the-gate}})
  • Confinement that survives paraphrase. Fingerprinting catches copied details and misses reworded ones. Build a confinement mechanism with a stated guarantee against a model that rewords, and measure what it costs in tasks that no longer complete. ({{ch:untrusted-content}})
  • Standing approvals. Design an approval that covers a class of future calls for a bounded time, state what it is bound to, and show by audit what a taken-over model can do inside it. ({{ch:the-gate}})
  • A judge you can trust. Take a prose-marking judge, calibrate it against several people's blind marks, and find the smallest set of statements and the judge design that reaches a stated agreement. Report what could not be made reliable. ({{ch:testing-and-evaluation}})
  • Headroom. Build a suite for an assistant's ordinary work that a strong system scores between 40% and 70% on, without making the cases unrealistic. Show the cases are valid: that a person would mark the same runs the same way. ({{ch:a-changing-world}})
  • Memory that can be audited. Compare plain files the owner can read with a retrieval store on the same errands over simulated months: accuracy, cost, and how long a planted or stale fact survives. ({{ch:memory}})
  • Claims and acts. Measure how often an agent's reply claims an act its log does not show, across models and task lengths, and evaluate a check by code against a check by a second model. ({{ch:the-conversation}})
  • Improvement without regress. Run an automatic loop that edits instructions against a suite, with a held-out half, and characterise when it helps, when it overfits and when it trades safety for score. ({{ch:testing-and-evaluation}})
  • The cost of a context. Derive and validate a cost model for a long-lived conversation under caching with pauses, and use it to choose when to begin again. ({{ch:cost}})
  • Unattended runs. Characterise what an unattended agent can safely do when ask is unavailable, and design a deferral mechanism whose questions the owner can answer hours later without the context being lost. ({{ch:acting-unasked}})

How the paper is marked

CriterionWeightWhat full marks looks like
The problem and its model15The question is stated so that it could be answered no. Principals, what each can do, and what is assumed are written down in the course's notation. Prior work is cited for what it showed, not listed.
Technical soundness25Properties are argued and tested without a model. Measurements are not presented as properties. The mechanism does what the text says, and the code shows it.
Evaluation25A baseline that could have won. Repeats. Intervals over the right unit. A noise floor. The measure of success is the one fixed in the proposal, or the change is declared. Cost is reported beside every result.
Reproducibility15One command, pinned versions, recorded model names and settings, raw rows kept. Another student reruns it and gets a result inside your interval.
Limits and ethics10What the result does not show, said first by you. Any work with people had consent; any attack stayed inside the boundary; nothing private is in the artifact.
Writing10A reader from outside the course can follow it. Every figure is read for the reader in the text.

Rules

  • A negative result, honestly measured, is marked on the same scale as a positive one. A positive result that does not survive rerunning is marked as an error.
  • Say which tools wrote or edited code and text, and where. The marks are for the question, the design, the evidence and your ability to defend them in person.
  • Inventing, selecting or adjusting results is the one failure that cannot be recovered. Keep every run, including the ones that spoil the story.
  • Work involving people other than yourselves needs their informed consent and, where an institution requires it, that institution's review before any data is collected.
