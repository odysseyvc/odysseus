How Odysseus is built

A personal AI agent you message on Telegram, which carries out errands on your behalf and asks before it does anything you cannot undo. This site records how it is being built, from nothing, so that a careful reader could build one too.

Status on 18 November 2026. Odysseus answers its owner in Telegram, searches the web, remembers facts between conversations, sends reminders and runs routines, reads mail and calendar, can draft, send and archive mail and create, change and delete events, can hand a task to a browsing agent, reads photos and documents, and asks for approval with a button before anything that reaches another person or cannot be undone. The parts that read have each run once against the real services. The parts that act have run only in a test world: no real email has been sent and no reminder has fired at its real time. It runs on its owner's laptop, because the one attempt at a server was stopped by the hosting service after five minutes. It now has a measurement. Chapter 14 describes a first errand suite that scored 97% and why that figure was weak evidence. Chapter 15 describes what research on evaluation found and the version built from it, in which the same errands fail one time in eleven once they are reworded and tools start failing.

What this site is

Three products set the standard for assistants of this kind in 2026: Instinct, Muse and Grok Bot. Each lets you send a message such as "move my dentist appointment to Friday and tell my sister" and have it done. Odysseus is an attempt to build something that works the same way, with Telegram as its first channel.

The chapters are written like a textbook: each one states what you should be able to do after reading it, defines its terms before using them, works through examples and ends with questions. They are revised as the build goes on, and each carries the date it was last revised.

One rule governs every chapter. Every factual claim is graded by how it is known: read at the source, reported second-hand, claimed by the company itself, or not yet tested. The grades are explained in How to read the evidence, and it is worth reading that page first.
