How Odysseus is built

A personal AI agent you message on Telegram, which carries out errands on your behalf and asks before it does anything you cannot undo. This site records how it is being built, from nothing, so that a careful reader could build one too.

Status on 17 November 2026. The research is done, the parts are chosen, and the first slice of code is written and running. It answers its owner in Telegram, searches the web, remembers facts about its owner between conversations, sends reminders and runs routines at set times, reads its owner's mail and calendar, can hand a task to a browsing agent, reads photos and documents its owner sends, asks for approval with a button before sending anything or setting up anything that recurs, stops on command and limits its own spending. Live runs costing about forty cents in all confirmed three assumptions, exposed one bug in how cost was counted and showed replies were slow to start; both are fixed. Memory and scheduling have each passed their test in one scripted run; neither has been used over days, and no scheduled job has yet fired at its real time. Mail and calendar are connected and have been read; a request to send was held for approval and declined, and nothing has yet been sent. The browser has visited two public pages and returned one doubtful report; nothing asks before a browser run or before a click inside one, by the owner's choice, which Chapter 11 discusses. File reading has been tried on one invented test page and on no real photograph. It was deployed to an always-on server on 17 November and stopped by the hosting service after five minutes, because the account has no payment card; it is back on its owner's laptop until that is settled. It can connect other apps from the chat by sending its owner a link, and has not yet done any work in one. No claim is made about how well it performs. When that changes, this page will say so with numbers.

What this site is

Three products set the standard for assistants of this kind in 2026: Instinct, Muse and Grok Bot. Each lets you send a message such as "move my dentist appointment to Friday and tell my sister" and have it done. Odysseus is an attempt to build something that works the same way, with Telegram as its first channel.

The chapters are written like a textbook: each one states what you should be able to do after reading it, defines its terms before using them, works through examples and ends with questions. They are revised as the build goes on, and each carries the date it was last revised.

One rule governs every chapter. Every factual claim is graded by how it is known: read at the source, reported second-hand, claimed by the company itself, or not yet tested. The grades are explained in How to read the evidence, and it is worth reading that page first.
