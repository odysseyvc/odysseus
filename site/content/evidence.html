How to read the evidence

A record of a build is only useful if the reader can tell what was observed from what was assumed. This page defines the four grades used on every page of the site.

Why grade claims at all

Writing about AI agents mixes three kinds of statement that look alike on the page: things someone measured, things a company said about its own product, and things that sound right. A reader who cannot tell them apart will copy mistakes. So each claim here carries a small label saying how it is known.

The four grades

LabelMeaningHow much to trust it
VerifiedRead on the vendor's own documentation, pricing page, changelog or source code on the date given, or measured by us with the method stated.High for what the page says. A pricing page proves the price, not the quality.
SecondaryReported by the press, a comparison site, an aggregator of leaderboards, or a vendor writing about a rival.Moderate. Often right, sometimes stale, occasionally written to sell something.
Maker's claimStated by the company about its own product, with no independent check. This includes benchmarks a company ran on itself.Treat as a hypothesis. Chapter 4 shows a claim of 93.4% that an independent test put at 32.4%.
Not testedPlanned, designed or written, but never run by us.None yet. It tells you what we intend, not what happens.

Three rules that follow

  1. Numbers come with their sample. "9 of 10" and "90%" are different statements. Where a count is known it is given.
  2. A result counts only when something other than the agent checked it. An agent that reports "done" has told you what it believes. Chapter 1 defines the stricter measure this project uses.
  3. Reversed decisions stay in the log. If a choice is changed, the earlier entry is not edited to look wiser. A new entry records the change and the reason.

Who is writing

Odysseus is being built by its owner working with an AI coding agent, which also drafts these pages from the project's research notes and test output. That agent runs on one of the vendors compared in Chapter 4. The conflict is declared there, next to the comparison it could bias.

The chapters refer to "the owner" and often cite "the build log" by entry number. The build log is the owner's private notebook, kept during the build, and is not published. A citation of it means the fact was recorded there at the time; the chapter states the fact itself in full.
